# Starting Nutri-Scan as a mechanical engineering student

**Start as a college project with one farm—not as a full company yet.**
Your first goal is to prove that the workflow is useful before spending heavily.

This plan is tailored to a mechanical engineering student at Sinhgad, Solapur.
No college endorsement, mentor, facility, funding or partnership is assumed.
The steps and 14-day target below are proposed, not completed activities.

## Five practical steps

1. **Find one faculty mentor.** Show the working synthetic-data demo and ask for
   guidance and farmer introductions. Ask about the college's entrepreneurship or
   incubation cell **if available**. Check college/institution IP rules before
   commercializing work or using college resources externally.

2. **Build a small team.** Use your mechanical skills for enclosure design, mounting,
   sample-handling design and field usability. Look for one electronics student for
   sensors and one software student for the app. Do not assume a teammate or module
   is already available; agree small responsibilities first.

3. **Speak to three dairy farmers.** Seek introductions through family, faculty or
   a local veterinarian. Use the [discovery guide](customer-discovery-interview.md):
   - How do you identify and check stored silage/feed?
   - How do you keep photos and laboratory reports?
   - Which follow-up task is regularly missed?
   Listen for recent examples; do not promise better milk yield or safe feed.
   Ask permission for notes and keep identifying information outside Git.

4. **Agree a small trial with one willing farmer.** Start with batch records,
   evidence photos, observation-only checks and in-app reminders—not a nutrient or
   safety test. You do not need expensive sensors to test this workflow. Use a
   synthetic demo initially; real farm data needs informed permission and the
   [technical readiness gates](../technical/technical-readiness-checklist.md).
   Agree who operates the app, the task being tested, duration and support.

5. **Choose hardware only after the need is clear.** Do not buy an expensive NIR
   instrument now. Actual nutrient/safety claims require suitable equipment,
   laboratory comparisons and expert validation. The ESP32 default/emulator values
   are synthetic and must be labelled; never use them to fill missing farm readings.

## Your next 14-day target

**One mentor + two teammates + three farmer interviews + one agreed small pilot.**

| Proposed window | Practical outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Days 1–3 | Show the demo to a potential mentor and ask for introductions. |
| Days 4–7 | Recruit teammates and arrange three permissioned farmer conversations. |
| Days 8–14 | Identify one repeated problem and agree a small pilot scope with a willing farm; document outstanding readiness gates before real use. |

This target is for discovery and agreement, **not automatic launch approval**.
If access, consent, security or support is unresolved, continue with synthetic demos
and planning rather than rushing real-data use to meet the date.

## Keep spending and promises small

- Use existing laptops/phones first. Agree a modest travel/materials budget with
  your team/mentor before spending; no grant or free college facility is assumed.
- Prove usefulness before major hardware purchases or company-formation spending;
  verify applicable requirements before selling or making commercial commitments.
- Consider charging only after scope, actual delivery costs, permissions, taxes and
  reviewed terms are clear. A later [six-week pilot proposal](pilot-proposal-template.md)
  can formalize the agreed workflow; it is not required for an initial conversation.
- Measure whether records are easier to retrieve and follow-ups are completed.
  Do not claim laboratory accuracy, toxin detection or certified feed safety.

For the wider business and technical plan, return to the
[launch guide](../README.md). Your first action is simply to arrange the mentor demo.
